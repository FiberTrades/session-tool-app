# Trading Academy — chart shot list

Sixteen charts. Each one replaces a drawn figure currently in a lesson.

Mark them up in TradingView, take the snapshot, and send me the link one at a time. I drop it
straight into the lesson — image support is live, and lesson images open full size on click.

**General notes**

- EUR/USD is fine for all of them, on whatever timeframe shows the shape most clearly.
- Annotate on the chart itself. Whatever you write becomes the teaching, so the wording is
  yours, not mine.
- The caption underneath each image is written in the lesson and is listed below, so the
  drawing only has to carry what the caption refers to.
- Where a lesson currently toggles between two or three states, each state is a separate
  image, listed separately.
- Nothing needs to be pretty. It needs to be **true** — that is the whole reason we are
  replacing mine.

## Module 3 — Reading the market

### 1. `3-1 structure` — a trend, then a break
**Lesson:** 3.1 Structure: highs, lows and broken

Must show a run of higher highs and higher lows, then price closing **below the last higher
low**.

Label: each swing as HH / HL, the last higher low as a level, and the point where it breaks.

Caption it supports: *"Price closed below the last higher low, so the trend is over. Taken
slowly a range is forming; taken with speed and closing beyond, it is an initiation."*

---

### 2. `3-2 range` — the range phase
**Lesson:** 3.2 The three phases

Must show price working between a premium and a discount, turning at both edges.

Label: the **premium** zone (top quarter), the **discount** zone (bottom quarter), and the
**50%** in the middle.

Caption it supports: *"The edges are where it is transacted. The 50% in the middle is the most
common trap in the method — no edge nearby to be wrong against, so nowhere honest to put a
stop."*

---

### 3. `3-2 trend` — the trend phase
**Lesson:** 3.2 The three phases

Must show higher highs and higher lows **twice over**, with each pullback reaching the **50% of
the previous A2B leg**.

Label: the swings, and the 50% level each pullback returns to.

Caption it supports: *"That second confirmation is what makes it a trend. Each pullback comes
back to the 50% of the leg before it, and that is where the entry is looked for, never on the
push."*

---

### 4. `3-2 initiation` — the initiation phase
**Lesson:** 3.2 The three phases

Must show a range, then an aggressive break with the **break candle closing strongly outside**
it, a **shallow** pullback to a small pocket at the break — explicitly not to the 50% — and
then extension away.

Label: the range, the break candle, the pocket it pulls back to.

Caption it supports: *"It does not return to the edge or to the 50% — only to a small mitigation
pocket at the break — and then it extends toward the next liquidity."*

---

### 5. `3-3 reaction` — the reaction entry
**Lesson:** 3.3 Reaction versus continuation

Must show: a high taken, a range mitigated in premium, price slowing there (small candles /
doji), and the entry taken **at the mitigation, before any confirmation candle**.

Label: the high that was taken, the mitigated range, the entry, the safe stop above the range.

Caption it supports: *"The entry is here, before anything confirms it. Less confirmation, more
risk, more reward for being early."*

---

### 6. `3-3 continuation` — the continuation entry
**Lesson:** 3.3 Reaction versus continuation

Same lead-in as the reaction chart, then: an **aggressive leg** that confirms direction, a small
range or doji forming at the **50% of that leg**, and the entry on the pullback into it.

Label: the aggressive leg, the small range at its 50%, the entry, the safe stop above it.

Caption it supports: *"The leg down came first and proved the direction. More confirmation, less
reward — and the one the method wants for a second trade."*

---

### 7. `3-4 higher timeframe` — location, not timing
**Lesson:** 3.4 HTF context, LTF trigger

A higher timeframe chart with **one area marked** as the only place worth caring about.

Label: the area.

Caption it supports: *"This tells you where, and it does not tell you when."*

---

### 8. `3-4 lower timeframe` — the same area, close up
**Lesson:** 3.4 HTF context, LTF trigger

The lower timeframe view **inside that same area**, ideally the same session as chart 7 so the
pair reads as one idea at two scales.

Label: that this is the inside of the area marked above.

Caption it supports: *"The lower one shows the moment inside it, and only inside it — the same
shape anywhere else means nothing."*

---

### 9. `3-5 liquidity` — the sweep
**Lesson:** 3.5 Liquidity

Must show **two or three highs at almost the same price**, then a push just above them, then a
reversal away.

Label: the equal highs, the band just above them where the stop orders rest, and the sweep.

Caption it supports: *"Price took the highs, filled every order waiting above them, and turned.
That push was not a breakout — it was the market reaching the orders it needed."*

## Module 4 — Building a session bias

### 10. `4-1 a prediction`
**Lesson:** 4.1 What a bias is, and is not

A chart with a single arrow or line to a target — one outcome, nothing else drawn.

Label: something like *"it goes here"*.

Caption it supports: *"One line, one outcome, and nothing to do if it does not happen."*

---

### 11. `4-1 a bias`
**Lesson:** 4.1 What a bias is, and is not

The **same chart**, marked up as a bias instead: an **area**, a **condition**, and an answer for
both cases.

Label: something like *"if it returns here and holds, I buy — if not, I do nothing today"*.

Caption it supports: *"An area, a condition, and an answer for both cases. This can be followed
on a day it works and a day it does not."*

---

### 12. `4-4 news`
**Lesson:** 4.4 News and the calendar

A real high-impact release on the chart: quiet beforehand, the spike, the move straight back
through where it started, then a readable chart again afterwards.

Label: the release, and the minutes either side of it.

Caption it supports: *"It moved, then moved straight back through where it started. Spreads
widen, stops fill at prices that were never on your chart, and none of this is a setup."*

---

## Module 5 — Execution

### 13. `5-1 RICE: Range`
**Lesson:** 5.1 RICE, the setup framework

The **R** of a full RICE sequence. Mark the range; leave the rest of the move visible but
unannotated so the four images read as one chart in four stages.

Caption: *"Price builds a balanced area between a premium and a discount. Liquidity settles
above and below."*

---

### 14. `5-1 RICE: Initiation`
**Lesson:** 5.1 RICE, the setup framework

Same chart, now marking the **I**: the break with speed, the candle closing strongly outside the
range, the liquidity taken on the way through.

Caption: *"This is the commitment — and it is not your entry."*

---

### 15. `5-1 RICE: Correction` — **this is the entry**
**Lesson:** 5.1 RICE, the setup framework

Same chart, marking the **C**: price coming back and **mitigating** the range it broke from.

This is the important one of the four. It has to show the *moment of mitigation* as the entry —
not a pullback developing and being waited out. Mark the mitigated range, the entry at the
mitigation, and the safe 2–4 pip stop above or below that range, so it is obvious that the stop
fitting is what makes it a trade.

Caption: *"That is the entry — the moment the range is mitigated and a safe stop fits above or
below it. No safe stop, no trade."*

---

### 16. `5-1 RICE: Extension` — **not an entry**
**Lesson:** 5.1 RICE, the setup framework

Same chart, marking the **E**: price resuming, and the target at the next liquidity.

Deliberately **no entry marker here.** The point of this image is that you were already in from
the correction, and that anyone entering once the extension is obvious has missed most of the
twenty pips.

Caption: *"You are already in — this is what the entry in the correction was positioned for."*
